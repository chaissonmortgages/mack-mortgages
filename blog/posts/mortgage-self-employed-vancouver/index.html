Self-Employed in Vancouver? Here's Why Your Mortgage Qualifying Looks Different

September 13, 2026 · Mack Chaisson, Licensed Mortgage Broker Self-Employed

You built a business, you're paying your bills, and your bank account looks healthy. But when you sit down with a lender and hand over your tax returns, the number they use to qualify you looks nothing like what you actually earn. This is one of the most common frustrations Mack Chaisson hears from self-employed buyers across Metro Vancouver, whether they're looking at a condo in Mount Pleasant, a townhouse in South Surrey, or a detached home in East Van. The good news is that the qualifying process for self-employed borrowers has more options than most people realize.

Why Your Write-Offs Can Work Against You at Qualification Time

This is the central tension in almost every self-employed mortgage conversation. The same legitimate business deductions that reduce your tax bill, vehicle expenses, home office costs, equipment, travel, and professional fees, also reduce the income figure lenders use to decide how much they'll lend you.

For most fully verified income programs, a lender is looking at your net income after deductions as reported on your Notice of Assessment, often averaged across the last two years. If your gross revenue is strong but your write-offs are aggressive, the qualifying number can drop significantly. Under standard guidelines, your Gross Debt Service ratio must stay at or below 39% and your Total Debt Service ratio at or below 44%. A lower qualifying income makes those thresholds harder to meet, especially in a market where Metro Vancouver property values mean larger mortgage amounts are the norm.

This does not mean you did anything wrong. It just means a traditional fully-verified approach may not reflect your real financial position, and that's where alternative programs come in.

Stated Income Programs: What They Are and What Lenders Actually Want to See

A stated income program allows a lender to use a reasonable, supportable income figure rather than your line 15000 NOA number. This is not a free pass. Lenders offering these programs are still doing their homework, and they typically want a clear picture of your business before they move forward.

Here is what most lenders on a stated income file are looking to verify:

  • Business registration documents, such as your business licence or incorporation papers, to confirm the business is real and operating
  • GST/HST returns, which give lenders a reliable signal of actual revenue flowing through the business
  • Two years of self-employment history in the same field, though some lenders have flexibility on this depending on the file
  • A stated income figure that is reasonable relative to your industry and the size of your operation
  • Strong credit history, since lenders carrying more income risk on a stated file will look more closely at how you manage debt

The income you state still needs to make sense. A general contractor in Burnaby stating an income consistent with someone running a one-person operation is a very different conversation than one stating an income that looks out of step with what similar businesses earn.

Incorporated vs. Sole Proprietor: A Quick Note

If you operate through a corporation, lenders also want to see whether you're paying yourself a salary, taking dividends, or some combination of both. Each of those is treated differently at qualification, and the documentation required shifts accordingly. Sole proprietors tend to have a simpler paper trail but fewer options for structuring their income. Neither is automatically better. It depends on how your business is set up and what your returns actually show.

Down Payment and Insured Mortgages for Self-Employed Buyers

One practical note worth knowing: CMHC-insured mortgages are available to self-employed borrowers, though insurers apply their own guidelines on top of the lender's. If your down payment is under 20%, the insurer is part of the picture. For purchases at or above $1,000,000, a minimum 20% down payment is required regardless of employment type, which is a real threshold in many parts of Metro Vancouver.

Self-employed mortgage qualifying is genuinely more nuanced than a salaried file, but it is far from a closed door. Understanding which program fits your specific income structure is where the real work happens, and that starts with a clear look at your documents and your goals.
