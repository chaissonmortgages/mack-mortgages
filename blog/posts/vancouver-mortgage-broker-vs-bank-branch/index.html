Why a Vancouver Mortgage Broker Can Do Things Your Bank Branch Simply Cannot

September 9, 2026 · Mack Chaisson, Licensed Mortgage Broker Vancouver Mortgage Broker

You're sitting across from a mortgage specialist at your bank's Kerrisdale or Burnaby branch. They're friendly, the rate sounds reasonable, and you're tempted to just sign and move on. But that specialist works for one lender. They can only offer you what that lender has on the shelf that day. In Metro Vancouver, where a small difference in mortgage structure can mean a meaningful difference in total cost over a five-year term, that limitation is worth thinking about before you commit.

One Lender vs. the Whole Market

A licensed mortgage broker like Mack Chaisson works with a wide network of lenders, including major banks, credit unions, monoline lenders, and trust companies. Monoline lenders, in particular, are institutions that do nothing but mortgages. They tend to compete aggressively on both rate and product flexibility because mortgages are their entire business. Your bank branch has no reason to tell you those lenders exist.

In practical terms, this means a broker can compare products across that full network and bring you the options that actually fit your file, not just the options one institution happens to offer. For a purchase in a market like East Vancouver or a refinance on a Port Moody townhouse, the right lender for your specific income structure, property type, and timeline may not be the one whose ATM you use most often.

What a Broker Does That a Bank Employee Cannot

A bank mortgage specialist is a salaried or commissioned employee with a fixed product shelf. A broker is an independent professional with a legal obligation to act in your interest. Those are structurally different roles, and the day-to-day work reflects that.

Here is what Mack does for a file that a bank employee simply cannot replicate:

  • Shops your application across lenders simultaneously, rather than presenting one offer and hoping you accept it.
  • Explains product differences that actually matter, like prepayment privileges, portability terms, and penalty calculation methods. A closed mortgage from a monoline may carry different break costs than one from a major bank, and that matters if your plans change.
  • Structures the application to fit BC-specific rules, including stress test requirements (qualifying at the higher of your contract rate plus 2%, or 5.25%), GDS and TDS ratio limits (39% and 44% respectively), and minimum down payment tiers that apply to Metro Vancouver price points.
  • Flags programs you might qualify for, such as the CMHC-insured 30-year amortization option available to first-time buyers and new-build purchasers on properties up to $1,500,000, which can meaningfully affect monthly cash flow.
  • Coordinates with your lawyer, realtor, and the lender through to funding, so you are not chasing paperwork across three separate organizations on your own.

The BC Context Makes This More Important, Not Less

Metro Vancouver's price environment means more buyers are working with larger loan amounts, tighter qualifying ratios, and more complex files than buyers in smaller markets. A buyer purchasing a pre-sale condo in Richmond or a detached home in New Westminster may need a lender that understands strata documentation requirements, assignment clauses, or rental income treatment. Not every lender is comfortable with every property type, and not every bank branch has the flexibility to work around a more complex situation.

Beyond the mortgage itself, understanding how your financing fits alongside BC-specific costs, like Property Transfer Tax, which runs 1% on the first $200,000 and 2% on the portion up to $2,000,000, affects what you actually need at the table on closing day. A broker who works in this market regularly will factor that into the conversation from the start.

If you've been assuming your bank is showing you everything available, it's worth a second opinion. Your situation is specific, and the mortgage market is larger than any one institution's product menu.
