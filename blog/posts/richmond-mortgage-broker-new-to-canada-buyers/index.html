New to Canada and Buying in Richmond? Here's What Your Mortgage Actually Looks Like

September 10, 2026 · Mack Chaisson, Licensed Mortgage Broker Richmond

Richmond has one of the highest concentrations of newcomers to Canada in all of Metro Vancouver. Many arrive with strong finances, real savings, and a genuine plan to buy a home, whether that's a condo near Richmond Centre, a townhouse in Broadmoor, or a detached home closer to Steveston. But when they sit down with a bank, they often hear the same word: declined. Not because they aren't financially capable, but because standard qualification rules weren't designed with their situation in mind. This is where working with a Richmond mortgage broker can make a meaningful difference.

Why Standard Qualification Is Harder for New-to-Canada Buyers

Canadian lenders rely heavily on two things when assessing a mortgage application: domestic credit history and verifiable Canadian income. Newcomers often have limited or no Canadian credit file, even if they had excellent credit in their home country. Employment income can also look unusual, especially if someone recently started a new job, is still building their business, or receives income in a foreign currency from overseas.

The mortgage stress test applies regardless of your background. Every borrower must qualify at the higher of their contract rate plus 2%, or 5.25%. That floor is the same for everyone. But the credit and income documentation lenders require on top of that stress test is where things get complicated for newcomers, and where the type of lender you apply through matters a lot.

Banks have relatively rigid internal policies. Monoline lenders and some credit unions have programs specifically built for newcomers that allow for alternative credit evidence and more flexible income documentation. A broker with access to a wide lender panel can identify which of those options fits your situation rather than sending you to whichever window the bank assigns you to.

Down Payment, Insured Mortgages, and What Changes for Newcomers

The standard minimum down payment rules apply to newcomers the same as anyone else. You'll need at least 5% on the first $500,000 of the purchase price, 10% on the portion between $500,000 and $999,999, and a full 20% minimum on any home priced at $1,000,000 or above. In Richmond, where detached homes frequently sit above the million-dollar threshold and even some condos push into the upper ranges, understanding exactly where your target price lands matters before you start shopping.

If you are a first-time buyer or purchasing a new build, the CMHC insured mortgage price cap of $1,500,000 becomes relevant. First-time buyers and new-build purchasers who qualify for insured financing may also access a 30-year amortization, which reduces your monthly payment and can help with qualifying under the stress test.

What counts as acceptable down payment for newcomers?

This is one of the most practical questions that comes up. Most lenders want to see the funds in a Canadian account, with a clear paper trail. For newcomers, that often means documenting a foreign transfer and showing the money has landed and settled. The requirements vary by lender, and getting the documentation organized correctly before you apply can prevent unnecessary delays or declines.

Practical Steps Worth Taking Before You Apply

Many newcomers in Richmond find it helpful to work through a checklist before approaching any lender. A few concrete things to prepare:

  • Open a Canadian bank account and start building a credit file as early as possible, even a basic secured credit card helps establish history
  • Gather your most recent employment letter, pay stubs, and if self-employed, any Canadian business registration and financial records you have
  • Collect documentation of your foreign income or assets if they are part of your qualifying picture
  • Have a clear record of where your down payment is coming from and be ready to show its movement into a Canadian account
  • Understand your residency status and how it affects lender eligibility, permanent residents and those on certain work permits qualify differently

Getting these items in order before your first lender conversation puts you in a stronger position and avoids the situation where a rushed application leaves a mark on your credit file without producing a useful result.

Everyone's situation is genuinely different, and the specifics of your income, residency status, and target property type in Richmond all shape which lenders and which programs make sense.
